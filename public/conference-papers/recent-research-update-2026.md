## New Recent Research Added (Within 4 Years)

### 1. **Empirical Studies on AI Tutoring Efficacy (2025-2026)**

**Lo et al. (2025)** - *Scientific Reports*
- **Study:** Randomised controlled trial of LLM tutors in K-12 mathematics
- **Finding:** AI tutors outperformed in-class active learning with substantial effect sizes
- **Significance:** First large-scale RCT demonstrating practical efficacy of LLM-based tutoring
- **Added to:** Section 2.4, Related Work - Intelligent Tutoring Systems

**Xu et al. (2026)** - *British Journal of Educational Technology*
- **Study:** Meta-analysis of 35 empirical studies (2013-2025) on AI support for SRL
- **Finding:** AI scaffolds aligned with Zimmerman's cyclical model significantly improved SRL processes and learning outcomes
- **Significance:** Decade-long synthesis showing strongest effects with adaptive AI support
- **Added to:** Section 2.4, Related Work - Intelligent Tutoring Systems

### 2. **Systematic Reviews on Cognitive Impact (2025)**

**Chen et al. (2025)** - *Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence*
- **Study:** Systematic review of 67 empirical studies on ChatGPT's cognitive impact (2022-2025)
- **Findings:** 
  - Mixed effects on critical thinking
  - Significant concerns about over-reliance and diminished effortful reasoning
  - Pattern: improved task completion but reduced unassisted transfer performance
- **Significance:** Most comprehensive synthesis of early ChatGPT education research
- **Added to:** Section 2.4, Section 2.6, References, Related Work

### 3. **Cognitive Offloading Research (2026)**

**Zhang et al. (2026)** - *arXiv preprint*
- **Study:** Examination of cognition, agency, and epistemic development in AI-assisted learning
- **Findings:** 
  - Cognitive offloading reduces opportunities for effortful reasoning
  - Contributes to illusory learning and overtrust
  - Agency depends critically on interaction design
- **Significance:** Links offloading to epistemic development concerns
- **Added to:** Section 2.6, References

**Kim & Lee (2026)** - *Computers in Human Behavior*
- **Study:** "The silent skill erosion: Cognitive offloading in the age of educational AI"
- **Finding:** Short-term performance improvements mask long-term learning degradation
- **Significance:** Documents the "performance paradox" empirically
- **Already in references, reinforced in text**

### 4. **Learner Agency Research (2026)**

**Dai et al. (2026)** - *Computers & Education*
- **Study:** Framework for redefining and measuring student agency in AI-assisted learning
- **Findings:** 
  - Agency dimensions: autonomy in tool selection, control over outputs, critical evaluation capacity
  - Agency not uniformly enhanced by AI tools
  - Depends critically on interaction design and pedagogical scaffolding
- **Significance:** First operationalised framework for measuring AI learner agency
- **Added to:** Section 2.5, References

### 5. **Metacognitive Scaffolding Research (2024-2025)**

**Kazemitabaar et al. (2024)** - *arXiv preprint*
- **Study:** Scaffolding metacognition in programming education through student-AI interactions
- **Findings:** 
  - Step-by-step pseudo-code scaffolds encourage reflection
  - Overly complete solutions discourage metacognitive processes
  - Structured note-taking and reflection improve transfer performance
- **Significance:** Design implications for AI scaffolding in technical domains
- **Added to:** References, Related Work - Digital Gardens

**Alsaiari et al. (2026)** - *Education Sciences*
- **Study:** AI as Scaffold or Shortcut? Metacognitive Regulation in Graduate Students' Educational App Design
- **Finding:** Metacognitive prompts encourage deeper engagement than answer-focused feedback
- **Significance:** Empirical support for reflection-before-correction design principle
- **Added to:** References (via search results)

### 6. **AI Writing Assistant Research (2024-2025)**

**Airaj (2024)** - *Springer*
- **Study:** Exploring students' generative AI-assisted writing processes
- **Findings:** 
  - Native and non-native speakers experience different dilemmas
  - Tension between efficiency gains and autonomy concerns
  - Critical thinking and self-regulation impacted
- **Significance:** Cross-cultural perspective on AI writing assistance
- **Added to:** Related Work - AI Scaffolding Systems

**Additional writing studies (2025)** - *ScienceDirect*
- **Study:** Impact of AI writing assistants on academic writing performance
- **Finding:** Within-subjects design with 44 students examining subjectivity, autonomy, motivation
- **Significance:** Empirical evidence of AI's impact on writing processes
- **Referenced in search results**

## Key Insights from Recent Research

### What We Now Know (2022-2026)

1. **AI Tutoring Efficacy:** LLM-based tutors can outperform traditional active learning when properly designed (Lo et al., 2025)

2. **SRL Alignment:** AI scaffolds work best when aligned with established SRL models like Zimmerman's cyclical framework (Xu et al., 2026)

3. **Cognitive Offloading Risk:** Systematic evidence confirms the "performance paradox" - improved task performance masks diminished durable learning (Chen et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026)

4. **Agency Measurement:** Learner agency can be operationalised and measured, but depends on interaction design (Dai et al., 2026)

5. **Metacognitive Scaffolding:** Structured reflection prompts are more effective than answer provision (Kazemitabaar et al., 2024)

6. **Writing Process Impact:** AI writing assistants create tensions between efficiency and autonomy that vary by learner characteristics (Airaj, 2024)

## Implications for AI Wiki Companion Design

The recent empirical research strongly supports the proposed design principles:

✅ **Learner Ownership (DP1):** Dai et al. (2026) confirms agency depends on learner control  
✅ **Scaffold Rather Than Substitute (DP2):** Kazemitabaar et al. (2024) shows complete solutions harm metacognition  
✅ **Reflection Before Correction (DP3):** Alsaiari et al. (2026) demonstrates deeper engagement with prompts  
✅ **Continuous Knowledge Integration (DP4):** Xu et al. (2026) shows adaptive scaffolding improves SRL
